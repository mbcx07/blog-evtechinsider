The Three-Row EV Showdown: Kia EV9, Rivian R1S, and Tesla Model X in the 2026 Family Test

2026-06-23 · EV Reviews · Tags: evtechinsider, ev reviews

The electric vehicle market has reached a pivotal moment. While early adopters were content with compact sedans and hatchbacks, the mainstream consumer demands space, versatility, and the ability to carry a family without compromise. Enter the three-row electric SUV—a segment that has exploded in the last two years, offering everything from budget-friendly options to luxury behemoths. In this comprehensive review, we pit three of the most compelling contenders against each other: the 2026 Kia EV9, the Rivian R1S, and the Tesla Model X. Each represents a different philosophy—value, adventure, and tech—but all promise to replace the gas-guzzling minivan or large SUV in your driveway. We'll dive deep into battery tech, real-world range, charging infrastructure, interior practicality, and driving dynamics to help you decide which one deserves your family's hard-earned money.

The New Family Standard: Why Three-Row EVs Are Taking Over

For years, the electric vehicle narrative was dominated by range anxiety and charging scarcity. But as battery chemistry has evolved and charging networks have expanded, the conversation has shifted to lifestyle. Families are no longer willing to sacrifice space or utility for the sake of zero emissions. The three-row EV segment has emerged as the answer, offering seating for six or seven, generous cargo capacity, and the latest in safety and connectivity. The 2026 models we're testing here are not just electric versions of existing SUVs—they are purpose-built platforms that leverage the benefits of skateboard chassis, low centers of gravity, and instant torque. The Kia EV9, built on Hyundai's E-GMP platform, brings 800V architecture and ultra-fast charging to the masses. The Rivian R1S, with its rugged off-road pedigree, offers a unique blend of adventure and luxury. And the Tesla Model X, now in its third generation, continues to push the envelope with falcon-wing doors and over-the-air updates. But which one truly delivers the best family experience? Let's break it down.

Battery and Range: The Numbers That Matter

Battery technology is the heart of any EV, and these three models showcase different approaches. The Kia EV9 uses a 99.8 kWh nickel-manganese-cobalt (NMC) battery pack, offering an EPA-estimated range of up to 304 miles on the rear-wheel-drive Long Range trim. Its 800V architecture allows for 10-80% charging in just 24 minutes on a 350 kW DC fast charger—a figure that rivals far more expensive vehicles. The Rivian R1S, in its standard Large pack configuration, comes with a 135 kWh battery (though a 149 kWh Max pack is available), delivering an estimated range of 321 miles. Rivian's battery management system is sophisticated, but its charging speed tops out at 200 kW, meaning a 10-80% session takes about 40 minutes. The Tesla Model X, with its 100 kWh battery, offers a range of up to 348 miles on the Long Range trim, and thanks to Tesla's extensive Supercharger network, it remains the benchmark for road-trip convenience. However, Tesla's charging speed is capped at 250 kW, and the Model X's efficiency is slightly lower than the EV9's due to its larger footprint and heavier curb weight. In real-world testing, the EV9's efficiency shines—it consistently achieves over 3.2 miles per kWh in mixed driving, while the R1S and Model X hover around 2.8-3.0 miles per kWh. For families, the EV9's combination of decent range and rapid charging makes it the most practical for long journeys, provided you have access to 350 kW chargers.

Charging Reality: From Home to Road Trip

Range is only half the story; charging infrastructure and speed determine whether you'll actually enjoy that cross-country trip. The Tesla Model X has the undeniable advantage of the Supercharger network, which now boasts over 50,000 stalls globally, with 250 kW peak speeds and seamless plug-and-charge functionality. Tesla's route planner is also the most mature, automatically factoring in elevation, temperature, and traffic. The Rivian R1S, while not as extensive, has been rapidly expanding its Adventure Network, with 3,500+ fast chargers planned by 2026, and it also supports CCS and Tesla's NACS adapter (with access to Superchargers starting in 2025). However, the R1S's 200 kW peak charging rate means longer stops—a 20-minute charge adds about 140 miles, whereas the Model X can add 200 miles in the same time. The Kia EV9, despite its impressive 350 kW capability, is often limited by the availability of such high-power chargers. In practice, most CCS stations top out at 150-250 kW, so the EV9's advantage is only realized on the newest 350 kW units. Still, the EV9's battery preconditioning and smart route planning (via the Kia Access app) help mitigate this. For home charging, all three support Level 2 AC charging at 11-19 kW, but the EV9 and R1S come with standard 11 kW chargers, while the Model X offers a 16.5 kW onboard charger as an option. If you're a family that primarily charges at home and takes occasional road trips, the Model X's network is the safest bet, but the EV9's faster charging on paper makes it a compelling alternative for those who live near high-speed corridors.

Interior and Practicality: Seating, Cargo, and Tech

Families care about space, comfort, and the little things that make daily life easier. The Kia EV9 is a masterclass in packaging. Its flat floor and long wheelbase (122 inches) allow for three rows of genuine adult-sized seating, with the third row offering 32.8 inches of legroom—enough for a 6-foot-tall passenger on short trips. The second-row captain's chairs (optional) slide and recline, and the EV9's cargo space is generous: 20.2 cubic feet behind the third row, 45.5 with the third row folded, and 81.7 with all rows down. The interior is minimalist but functional, with a 12.3-inch dual-screen setup, physical climate controls, and a host of USB-C ports. The Rivian R1S takes a more premium approach, with vegan leather, wood accents, and a panoramic glass roof. Its third row is slightly tighter (30.1 inches of legroom), but the R1S compensates with a gear tunnel and a front trunk (frunk) that adds 11 cubic feet of storage. The R1S also offers a unique "camp mode" that levels the suspension and provides a tent attachment—perfect for outdoor families. The Tesla Model X, with its falcon-wing doors, is the most dramatic, but those doors can be problematic in low garages and reduce headroom for third-row access. The Model X's third row is the smallest of the three (29.1 inches of legroom), and its cargo space is 18 cubic feet behind the third row, 42 with it folded, and 88 with all seats down. However, the Model X wins on tech: a 17-inch touchscreen, over-the-air updates, and the best voice recognition in the business. For families with kids, the EV9's practical layout and physical buttons (a rarity in EVs) make it the most user-friendly, while the R1S offers the most versatility for active lifestyles.

Performance and Driving Feel: Not Just a Minivan Replacement

One of the biggest surprises in the three-row EV segment is how well these vehicles handle. The Kia EV9, even in its base rear-wheel-drive form, delivers 201 horsepower and 258 lb-ft of torque—enough for a 0-60 mph time of 7.7 seconds, which is adequate but not thrilling. The dual-motor all-wheel-drive version boosts that to 379 hp and 443 lb-ft, cutting the sprint to 5.3 seconds. The EV9's suspension is tuned for comfort, with a soft ride that absorbs bumps effortlessly, but it can feel floaty on highways. The Rivian R1S, in its quad-motor configuration, produces a staggering 835 horsepower and 908 lb-ft of torque, launching from 0-60 mph in 3.1 seconds—supercar territory for a 7,000-pound SUV. Its adaptive air suspension and hydraulic roll control make it surprisingly agile, with precise steering and minimal body roll. The R1S also offers adjustable ride height, from 8.1 inches in sport mode to 14.9 inches in off-road mode. The Tesla Model X, in Plaid trim, is even quicker at 2.5 seconds to 60 mph, but the standard Long Range version still manages 3.8 seconds. The Model X's air suspension is firm and sporty, but its steering is numb compared to the R1S. For most families, the EV9's balanced performance is more than sufficient, while the R1S and Model X cater to those who want a thrill. However, the R1S's off-road capability is unmatched—it can ford 3 feet of water and climb 45-degree slopes—making it the only true adventure vehicle here. The Model X, with its low center of gravity, feels more like a sports car, but its falcon-wing doors and complex mechanisms can be a source of reliability concerns.

The Verdict: Which One Wins the Family Garage?

After weeks of testing, it's clear that there is no single "best" three-row EV—it depends on your priorities. The Kia EV9 is the value champion, offering 90% of the capability at a starting price of $56,395 (before tax credits), with a spacious interior, rapid charging, and a 10-year/100,000-mile warranty. It's the sensible choice for families who want a no-nonsense electric SUV that does everything well. The Rivian R1S, starting at $75,900, is the adventurer's pick, with unmatched off-road prowess, a premium interior, and a community-driven brand ethos. It's more expensive and less efficient, but it's also more unique and capable. The Tesla Model X, starting at $79,990, is the tech and network king, with the best charging infrastructure, the most advanced software, and the quickest acceleration. However, its third row is cramped, and its build quality has historically been inconsistent. For the average family, the EV9 offers the best balance of price, practicality, and charging speed. But if you have the budget and crave adventure, the R1S is a revelation. And if you're deeply invested in the Tesla ecosystem, the Model X remains a compelling, if flawed, choice. As the EV market matures, we expect to see even more three-row options from Hyundai, Ford, and Chevy (the Silverado EV is a truck, but its SUV sibling is coming). For now, the EV9, R1S, and Model X represent the pinnacle of family electric transportation—each proving that going green doesn't mean sacrificing space, comfort, or fun.

Conclusion

The three-row EV segment is no longer a niche—it's the battleground where automakers are winning over the most important demographic: families. The Kia EV9, Rivian R1S, and Tesla Model X each bring unique strengths to the table, and our testing reveals that the best choice depends on your lifestyle, budget, and tolerance for compromise. The EV9 is the pragmatic hero, the R1S is the rugged explorer, and the Model X is the tech-savvy speedster. As battery technology continues to improve and charging networks expand, the barriers to EV adoption are crumbling. By 2026, there's no reason to settle for a gas-guzzling minivan when you can have an electric SUV that's faster, safer, and more efficient. Whether you're carpooling to soccer practice or embarking on a cross-country road trip, these three EVs prove that the future of family transportation is electric—and it's here now.
